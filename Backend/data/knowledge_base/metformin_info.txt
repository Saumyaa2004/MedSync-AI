Metformin is a first-line medication used to treat type 2 diabetes. It works by reducing glucose production in the liver and improving the body's sensitivity to insulin.

Common side effects of metformin include nausea, diarrhea, stomach upset, and a metallic taste in the mouth. These side effects often improve after the first few weeks of treatment.

Patients are advised to take metformin with food to reduce gastrointestinal side effects. It is usually taken once or twice daily, depending on the prescribed dose.

Regular monitoring of kidney function and blood sugar levels is recommended for patients on long-term metformin therapy. Metformin should be used with caution in patients with kidney disease.

In rare cases, metformin can cause a serious condition called lactic acidosis. Symptoms include unusual muscle pain, difficulty breathing, stomach pain, and feeling unusually cold. Patients experiencing these symptoms should seek immediate medical attention.
