Diabetes is a chronic condition that affects how the body processes blood sugar (glucose). There are two main types: Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes.

Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition where the body produces little or no insulin. It is usually diagnosed in children and young adults and requires daily insulin injections.

Type 2 diabetes is the most common form, where the body does not use insulin properly. It is managed through lifestyle changes, oral medications like Metformin, and sometimes insulin.

Common symptoms of diabetes include frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, and slow-healing wounds.

Patients with diabetes should monitor their blood sugar regularly, follow a balanced diet low in refined carbohydrates and sugar, exercise regularly, and take medications as prescribed.

Long-term complications of uncontrolled diabetes include heart disease, kidney damage, nerve damage, and vision problems.
